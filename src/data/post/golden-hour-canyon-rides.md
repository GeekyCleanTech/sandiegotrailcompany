Ask any of our guides for their favorite time to ride and you'll get the same answer: the last hour before sunset. We call it golden hour for a reason.

## The light does the work

In the middle of the day, the canyon is beautiful but flat — bright, hot, a little washed out. As the sun drops, everything shifts. Shadows stretch across the trail, the hills turn amber and rose, and the dust kicked up by the horses hangs in the air like something out of a film. You don't need a filter. You barely need a camera.

For photographers — amateur or serious — a sunset ride is the easiest way to come home with images that look like a magazine spread. The warm, low light is flattering to everything: the horses, the landscape, and you.

## It's cooler, calmer, and quieter

Southern California afternoons can run hot. Riding into the evening means better temperatures for you and the horses, and the backcountry empties out as the day winds down. More often than not, it's just your small group, your guide, and open land in every direction.

There's also something about the end of the day that slows people down. Conversations get quieter. Phones stay in pockets. The ride becomes less about doing and more about being somewhere worth being.

## A few tips for sunset riders

- **Book early.** These slots go first, especially on weekends.
- **Bring a light layer.** It cools off quickly once the sun is down.
- **Give yourself time after.** Watching the last color fade from the ridgeline is half the experience.
